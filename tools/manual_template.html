The run

A run is a campaign of $subnets subnets. Each is $subnet_min of waves, then a distinct subnet boss. Defeating the boss does not end the subnet — it starts its collapse, and you have to walk out. Clearing the last subnet wins.

Movement is the only input

WASD or arrows. Every weapon fires on its own cadence. Screen speed is uniform in all directions — the isometric squash is corrected for, so a dodge is judged on screen rather than in world space.

Levels come from shards

Each kill drops shards. A level costs round((5 + 3(n−1)) × $xp_slow) — so $xp_seq, and up. A level-up offers three module cards; one click places one.

Death ends the campaign

Integrity carries across subnets; clearing one restores $heal%. Salvage banks at every subnet clear, so dying late keeps what the earlier subnets paid. Kills and flips always count toward unlocks.

Enemies harden as you do

Enemy integrity scales $hp_subsubnet−1 × (1 + $hp_over × elapsed/300). Without it, a rank buying damage linearly meant everything one-shot forever once your damage passed a firewall's.

The arena

Sentinel Array requires four captured spires. Worm.exe has separately damageable segments and four regeneration events at most; its head is the kill target. Root Cause changes from ambush to a warned barrage and then locked-direction charges.

Main arenas are now about 31% larger. Complete the subnet's network job to reveal an archive off the east service port. Reach its terminal for 100 shared salvage and a rank offer for every LIVE player. The archive pays once and collapses with the rest of the subnet.

Each subnet generates its own arena from the run seed, so a bug report reproduces exactly. Generation guarantees the field is fully connected: any region the flood fill cannot reach is filled in rather than carved open, because a sealed pocket is an unwinnable run.

Walls · $density% of the field

Small scattered blocks, the same density on every subnet. They stop you, your shots and the swarm; enemies steer around them but do not path, so one can still be pinned against a wall by the crowd behind it. Worms ignore terrain entirely.

Zones

Hazard costs $hazard integrity a second and burns enemies too, so kiting a swarm through one is a real play. Slow cuts speed to $slowpct%. Corruption adds $corrsec corruption a second to anything standing in it.

The teleporter

Defeating the boss activates the transfer pad. Gather every LIVE teammate inside its ring to vote on three destinations. The world pauses during the ballot; the winning route configures the next subnet, then the pad uploads the party to separate safe arrival points. Only the current subnet is loaded. The final boss wins directly.

The collapse · $collapse seconds

Defeating the boss stops all spawning and starts the arena coming apart, from the point farthest from the teleporter inward. Standing on ground that goes is fatal. The way out lights up as tiles across walkable ground, following the actual route rather than pointing through walls.

Untested tuning

The $collapse-second collapse clock was set when the arena was a fifth of its current size. Crossing $arena_w × $arena_h units to reach the gate may not fit in it. Treat the deadline as unverified.

Set-pieces & subnet bosses

One set-piece a minute, each with a mechanic no ordinary enemy has. None has to be killed — but they stay on the field, so ignoring one means fighting it alongside everything that spawns after it. A kill pays $mb_salvage salvage and a guaranteed card.

Two names, two things

fork_bomb is both a set-piece enemy and a payload module. They are unrelated: the payload adds area damage to a weapon, the enemy splits when killed. The tables are separate in code so nothing misbehaves, but the collision is confusing and one of them should be renamed.

The build

You hold $exploits exploits. Each is one weapon with three slots, and each slot takes exactly one module — so a module's slot type decides its column, and the only question a level-up card asks is which row.

Rank, not duplicates

Placing a module where it already sits ranks it up, to a maximum of 5. Ranks are per slot, so the same module in two exploits is two independent copies. Replacing a module destroys its rank — drawn again, it re-enters at 1.

Triggers pay on the axis their frequency suits

A frequent trigger is paid in cadence, because its value is rate. A rare one is paid in burst — how many shots one event produces. interval sits at 1.00: the baseline, and the only trigger that fires on an empty field.

Defensive stats take the max

Wards, lifesteal, slow and shield fold by maximum across slots, never by sum. The same module is legal in many slots, and summing a defensive magnitude would buy it at no uptime cost.

Corruption is a second bar

Damage tagged corruption fills it. An enemy that fills it flips into a botnet node fighting for you, and drops the same shards a kill does — so a corruption build does not starve its own levelling.

Modules

$n_unlocked of $n_modules are available from the start; the other $n_locked unlock against lifetime kill and flip counts, split so a corruption build and a damage build climb different ladders. Locked entries show their requirement.
