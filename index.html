The run

A run is a campaign of 3 subnets. Each is 5:00 of waves, then ICE. Killing ICE does not end the subnet — it starts its collapse, and you have to walk out. Clearing the last subnet wins.

Movement is the only input

WASD or arrows. Every weapon fires on its own cadence. Screen speed is uniform in all directions — the isometric squash is corrected for, so a dodge is judged on screen rather than in world space.

Levels come from shards

Each kill drops shards. A level costs round((5 + 3(n−1)) × 1.8) — so 9, 14, 20, 25, 31, and up. A level-up offers three module cards; one click places one.

Death ends the campaign

Integrity carries across subnets; clearing one restores 30%. Salvage banks at every subnet clear, so dying late keeps what the earlier subnets paid. Kills and flips always count toward unlocks.

Enemies harden as you do

Enemy integrity scales 1.55subnet−1 × (1 + 0.45 × elapsed/300). Without it, a rank buying damage linearly meant everything one-shot forever once your damage passed a firewall's.

The arena

Each subnet generates its own arena from the run seed, so a bug report reproduces exactly. Generation guarantees the field is fully connected: any region the flood fill cannot reach is filled in rather than carved open, because a sealed pocket is an unwinnable run.

Walls · 3% of the field

Small scattered blocks, the same density on every subnet. They stop you, your shots and the swarm; enemies steer around them but do not path, so one can still be pinned against a wall by the crowd behind it. Worms ignore terrain entirely.

Zones

Hazard costs 12 integrity a second and burns enemies too, so kiting a swarm through one is a real play. Slow cuts speed to 60%. Corruption adds 8 corruption a second to anything standing in it.

The gate

Stands at one edge from the moment the arena generates, shut and solid — you fight past it for the whole subnet. Killing ICE opens it. Beyond it a 1100-unit walkway runs out to the next subnet; reaching its far end is the transition, and there is no teleport.

The collapse · 75 seconds

ICE dying stops all spawning and starts the arena coming apart, from the point farthest from the gate inward. Standing on ground that goes is fatal. The way out lights up as tiles across walkable ground, following the actual route rather than pointing through walls.

Untested tuning

The 75-second collapse clock was set when the arena was a fifth of its current size. Crossing 2 × 7156 units to reach the gate may not fit in it. Treat the deadline as unverified.

Two names, two things

fork_bomb is both a set-piece enemy and a payload module. They are unrelated: the payload adds area damage to a weapon, the enemy splits when killed. The tables are separate in code so nothing misbehaves, but the collision is confusing and one of them should be renamed.

The build

You hold 3 exploits. Each is one weapon with three slots, and each slot takes exactly one module — so a module's slot type decides its column, and the only question a level-up card asks is which row.

Rank, not duplicates

Placing a module where it already sits ranks it up, to a maximum of 5. Ranks are per slot, so the same module in two exploits is two independent copies. Replacing a module destroys its rank — drawn again, it re-enters at 1.

Triggers pay on the axis their frequency suits

A frequent trigger is paid in cadence, because its value is rate. A rare one is paid in burst — how many shots one event produces. interval sits at 1.00: the baseline, and the only trigger that fires on an empty field.

Defensive stats take the max

Wards, lifesteal, slow and shield fold by maximum across slots, never by sum. The same module is legal in many slots, and summing a defensive magnitude would buy it at no uptime cost.

Corruption is a second bar

Damage tagged corruption fills it. An enemy that fills it flips into a botnet node fighting for you, and drops the same shards a kill does — so a corruption build does not starve its own levelling.
